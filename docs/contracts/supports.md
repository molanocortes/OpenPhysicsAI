# Contract: support structures as typed physics (T16)

Up: [lpbf-build.md](lpbf-build.md) (section 11 is the overhang rule this builds on). Written 2026-09-19 **before the
code**, on the owner's direction (VISION.md, 2026-09-19): supports are physics, not a stiffness fudge; they change the
heat path and the mechanics; an agent must be able to choose and control every parameter.

## 1. What an agent controls

One operation, `lpbf_supports_generate`, takes a support **type** and its geometric parameters, each a quantity with a
unit and a provenance (`user`, `published`, `assumed`; a value without a source is refused, as every other input of the
build). It returns the support volume, the contact area on the part, the overhang faces it served, the ones it left
(steep, or standing on the part) and the homogenised properties it derived. `lpbf_build_run` then builds with that
support set; the FFF print (`print_run`) accepts the same set for its heat model and its stress history.

| type | geometry of the unit cell | parameters (besides the common ones) |
|---|---|---|
| `block` | a square grid of thin vertical walls | `wall_thickness`, `spacing` |
| `thin_wall` | parallel thin vertical walls | `wall_thickness`, `spacing`, `direction` (x or y) |
| `cone` | a cone per grid point, wide at the plate, narrow at the part | `base_radius`, `top_radius`, `spacing` |
| `tree` | a trunk per coarse grid point, branching to tips at the part | `trunk_radius`, `branch_radius`, `spacing` (tips), `trunk_spacing`, `branch_height` |
| `lattice` | a body-centred cubic strut lattice | `cell_size`, `strut_diameter` |

Common: `critical_angle` (the overhang threshold, section 11 of lpbf-build.md), `offset_from_part` (no support within
this horizontal distance of a part wall that is not an overhang), `height_above_plate` (places the part), `interface`
(`tooth_height`, `tooth_pitch`, `contact_fraction`: the top band of the support touches the part only on teeth),
`relative_density` (of the printed support material against the part), and `channels` (`excluded`, the rule; or
`supported`, the sensitivity). The scalar `stiffness_fraction` of wave 4 stays accepted as type `homogeneous`, so every
earlier result can be reproduced.

**Defaults and their sources.** The Simufact Additive 2025.1 tutorial (owner's copy, outside the repository, nothing
copied; printed page numbers) names the parameters but gives **no numeric default for any of them**: supports are
generated by CADS Additive, "a vast number of parameters" documented in infosheets that are not in the owner's files
(p. 54); the critical surface angle has no value (p. 55); shell supports carry a thickness that "directly influences the
supports stiffness", no value (p. 56); the relative material density of the supports lowers their stiffness, and
supports printed like the part are 100 % (p. 60, also p. 266: it matters without a HIP stage); the voxel stiffness is
scaled by the volume fraction (p. 72); a support radius exists, the example sets 0.45 mm (p. 224, an example, not a
default); part, supports and plate share nodes in the voxel mesh (pp. 228, 234); supports are removed in removal
stages, and unassigned ones are not removed (p. 176). Every default below is therefore **assumed** and says so in the
result.

## 2. Homogenisation: properties from the geometry

For a support type and its parameters the unit cell (one pitch in x and y) is voxelised finely enough that the thinnest
feature spans at least two voxels, and solved explicitly:

- **stiffness**: the cell is compressed vertically with its side faces held in their normal direction (the periodic
  condition of a cell in an array under uniaxial strain), hex8 elements of `src/fem/solid.c`. The fraction is the
  vertical stiffness of the cell over that of solid, `C33_cell / C33_solid`, and the lateral one `C11_cell / C11_solid`
  is reported with it. The build applies the vertical fraction as an isotropic scale (the support elements' modulus and,
  with plasticity, their yield stress); the anisotropy it ignores is stated in the result.
- **conduction**: a temperature difference across the cell, side faces insulated, finite volumes on the same voxels;
  vertical and lateral conductivity fractions. The heat model takes them as an orthotropic conductivity (the thermal
  material's principal values).
- **capacity**: the solid fraction of the cell times `relative_density`.

Types whose section changes with height (cone, tree, the tooth band) are solved slab by slab at the build's layer
height, and every support element takes the fractions of its own height. `relative_density` multiplies all three
(tutorial p. 72: the solver scales stiffness with the volume fraction).

## 3. Removal

Supports are removed as element death, after the cut and in one step with it when the cut frees the part (lpbf-build
section 12). The **interface tear-off** is stated: the force the supports carried into the part just before removal,
over the interface nodes, is written to the result: the largest nodal force and the sum of their magnitudes. It is the
load the part releases when they go. Its resultant is zero whenever the part stands on its supports only (the
eigenstrain loads the part by itself), which is the check, not the quantity.

## 4. Verification cases and criteria (registered before the code)

| id | case | criterion |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | a unit cell that is all solid | stiffness and conduction fractions 1 to 1e-9 |
| S2 | parallel walls, thickness t on pitch s | conduction fraction across the walls' plane (vertical) equals t / s to 1e-9; stiffness fraction equals (t / s) E / (1 - nu^2) / C33_solid within 1 % (a wall free on its faces, confined along its length) |
| S3 | every type, voxel size halved | fractions change by less than 5 % |
| S4 | the tooth band with contact fraction c | its conduction fraction is c times that of the band below it, to 1e-9 on voxels that resolve the teeth |
| S5 | the bridge of lpbf-build section 11 at 0.25 mm voxels, block supports (0.5 mm walls, 2 mm pitch), eigenstrain build: the supports meshed explicitly as walls against the homogenised support at the same voxels | largest displacement of the deck on the plate within **10 %**; largest displacement after removal within 10 % |
| S6 | the same bridge in the FFF heat model, explicit walls against homogenised | temperature rise above the bed at the deck's mid-span top at the end of printing within **5 %**; heat into the bed over the print within 5 % |
| S7 | the generator | a box on the plate gets none; the bridge's span only; a closed channel none (`channels: excluded`); `offset_from_part` keeps supports that distance from a vertical wall; volume and contact area equal the counted voxels times their fractions |
| S8 | the operation | a parameter without a unit or a provenance is refused; an unknown type is refused; every default used is listed as assumed in the result |
| S9 | removal | after removal no support element is active; for a part standing on its supports only, the resultant of the interface forces is zero to 1e-6 of their largest nodal value |

Criteria S5 and S6 decide whether the homogenised support may be used for a part: if either fails, the result says
so and the tolerance is not widened after the fact.

## 5. Results

### Amendment 2026-09-19 (before the run it adds): S5b

S5 as registered cannot discriminate: on the bridge the legs carry the part, and filling the gap solid instead of
with the block support moves the largest displacement by 0.9 % on the plate and 4.2 % after removal, inside the 10 %
that the correct homogenisation also meets. Added, with the same tolerance and before it was run: **S5b**, a slab
12 x 4 x 2 mm standing 4 mm above the plate on block supports alone (0.5 mm walls, 2 mm pitch, 0.25 mm voxels and
layers), explicit walls against the homogenised block: largest displacement on the plate and after removal (held
3-2-1) within 10 %. The solid fill is run beside it to show the criterion can fail.

### Results, 2026-09-19

**S1 to S4, the unit cells** (`build/mechtest`): an all-solid cell has every fraction 1 to 1e-9; parallel walls
conduct their area fraction exactly and are as stiff as `(t/s) E/(1-nu^2)` to 0.00 % (0.18935 of C33); halving the
voxels moves every type by less than 5 % at the default resolution (block 0.1 %, thin wall 0.0 %, cone 2.0 %, tree
1.3 % and 2.2 %, lattice 4.2 % and 2.2 %); the tooth band conducts 0.500000000 of the wall below it. Two things were
needed and are part of the method: voxels carry their solid volume fraction (4 x 4 x 4 samples) instead of a
staircase, which the inclined struts of tree and lattice need to converge; and the default resolution is four voxels
across a wall or cone, eight across a strut. The stiffness is the full orthotropic matrix by periodic homogenisation
(six macro strains, periodic fluctuation through the solver's constraint table), not one fraction.

**S5, the bridge** (explicit walls against the homogenised block, same 0.25 mm voxels, same 42.0 mm^3 of support):
largest displacement on the plate +0.31 %, after removal -0.40 %: **passes**. But it does not discriminate: the
solid fill passes too (-0.9 %, -4.2 %), because the legs carry the bridge.

**S5b, the slab on supports alone** (amendment above): the homogenised block misses the largest displacement on the
plate by **-23.5 % (fails)** and after removal by **+11.6 % (fails)**; with the earlier isotropic scaling it was
-20.8 % and +7.3 %; the solid fill -45.6 % and -15.7 %. The top surface agrees within 2 % everywhere on the plate
(-0.00309 mm at mid-span in both); the difference is local. The slab's underside dips at every wall line and rises
between them (-0.0067 against -0.0044 mm), and its edges, which overhang the outermost wall by 0.75 mm, curl up by
0.0072 mm against 0.0030 mm homogenised: the largest displacement sits at the corners, on that overhang. A
homogenised support has no pitch, so it cannot represent a part overhanging its last support or bending between
walls, and here the pitch (2 mm) equals the slab's thickness. After removal the freed slab's mid-span differs by 18 %
(-0.0173 against -0.0205 mm), which is the locked-in stress the continuous support leaves. What this means for use:
the homogenised support reproduces the part's field away from the supported face when the pitch is small against
the part, and not the extreme values at a supported face or an overhanging edge. The tolerance is not widened.

**S6, the bridge in the FFF heat model** (generic PLA, 0.25 mm voxels and layers, 210 degC nozzle, 60 degC bed,
explicit walls against the homogenised block of the same walls, same 42.0 mm^3 of support). First run, the support
exchanging heat with the air only through the outer faces of its voxels: the deck's top at mid-span ends the print
44.8 K above the bed against 35.3 K explicit (**+27 %, fails**), and 6.96 J flow into the bed during the print
against 2.04 J (**+241 %, fails**). The walls lose most of their heat through their own large surface to the air; a
continuum support has none, so its heat goes down into the bed and up into the deck.

Model change made because of that failure (the criterion unchanged): the unit cell also yields the pattern's surface
per volume (faces between voxels at least half full and the rest, periodic, the slab's cut faces excluded: 1.5 per mm
for 0.5 mm walls on a 2 mm pitch), and each support element exchanges heat over that area, convection with the film
coefficient and grey radiation with the material's emissivity, spread evenly over its six faces in place of its voxel
skin. Nothing is fitted: the area comes from the geometry, the coefficients are the process's.

After that change: the deck's rise **38.5 K against 35.3 K (+9.0 %, fails)** and the heat into the bed during the
print **4.24 J against 2.04 J (+108 %, fails)**. The change closes two thirds of the temperature gap and more than
half of the heat gap, and S6 stays failed at its registered 5 %. What is left is not explained yet: candidate causes,
not verified, are the contact between the support region and the legs (the homogenised block fills up to them, the
explicit walls along x stop 0.75 mm short, those along y touch them), the conduction into the deck through a
continuous face instead of wall lines, and the pitch again (2 mm walls under a 2 mm deck). The homogenised thermal
support is therefore not yet a verified model; its results are reported with this row next to them.

### S10, the cantilever's own comb as supports (run 2026-09-19, at the architect's request)

The calibration cantilever carries itself on its comb: 1 mm teeth on a 2 mm pitch, ribs across the full width. Every
result of this project meshes those teeth explicitly. S10 replaces them with this contract's supports, generated under
the beam by the overhang rule and homogenised from their unit cell, keeps this project's fitted strains (fitted on the
explicit-teeth model) and asks the pre-registered question of the validation protocol: do P14, P11 and P08 stay inside
**15 per cent** of the measurement? Run with a script of 40 s per model; the script, its records and the measurements
were removed from the repository on 2026-09-27, so the table below is the record of that run.

| model of the comb | P14 X | P14 Y | P11 X | P11 Y | P08 X | P08 Y |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| explicit teeth (the entry on the board) | +2.55 % | -3.18 % | +2.33 % | -1.16 % | +2.07 % | +0.10 % |
| **thin_wall supports, the comb's own pattern** | +82.6 % | +99.1 % | +78.6 % | +104.1 % | +74.0 % | +107.6 % |
| **block supports** | -80.8 % | -79.4 % | -81.2 % | -78.9 % | -81.7 % | -78.6 % |

**S10 fails, in both directions, and the unit cell says why.** For walls whose normal is x (the comb's ribs), the cell
gives C22 = C33 = 0.5611 E and G_yz = 0.188 E, all as expected for half-dense walls, and **G_zx = G_xy = 0**: an
infinite array of parallel plates, separated by gaps, has no shear stiffness across them, because each plate tilts
freely. A real tooth cannot: it is welded to the plate below and to the beam above, and it is 6 mm tall, three pitches.
The comb's stiffness is an end effect, which homogenisation removes by construction, so the beam curls about twice as
far. Block supports, whose walls run both ways, carry that shear and overshoot the other way by a factor of five.
**Conclusion, and the rule this contract now carries: a homogenised support may not replace a support structure whose
height is a few pitches or whose stiffness comes from its ends.** Where the support structure is part of the
calibration geometry, as the comb is, it stays meshed. This is the same limit S5b found (the largest displacement at a
supported face), measured here against a real measurement instead of another model.

**One change the run forced** (in the code, not in the criteria): a support's homogenised matrix now carries a
millionth of the solid's isotropic matrix beside it. Parallel walls carry nothing across their gaps and nothing in two
of the three shear modes, and an element whose matrix is singular has no stiffness to condense; the build refused to
assemble before it.
